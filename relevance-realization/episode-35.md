# Episode 35 - The Symbol, Sacredness, and the Sacred

## Overview

In this episode, Vervaeke explores the nature of symbols, their relationship to sacredness, and what constitutes "The Sacred." He distinguishes between the experience of sacredness (a psychological and cognitive phenomenon) and claims about The Sacred (metaphysical proposals about supernatural or absolute value).

## Key Concepts

### Symbols as Participatory Metaphors

Symbols are not mere signs that refer to something else. They are participatory in nature:

- **Exemplification over Reference**: Symbols don't just point to something; they participate in and exemplify what they represent
- **Activation vs Exaptation**: Symbols allow us to reverse the exaptation process - activating deeper cognitive machinery
- **Holding in Mind**: Symbols let us hold in mind things we normally cannot, enabling transformation

### The EPIC Nature of Symbols

Symbols are EPIC - they possess four key characteristics:

1. **Ek-stasis** (Ecstatic): Symbols help us "stand beyond ourselves," facilitating self-transcendence
2. **Participatory**: We don't just observe symbols; we engage with them, becoming transformed in the process
3. **Integrative** (Anagogic): Symbols integrate aspects of ourselves while simultaneously disclosing new aspects of reality
4. **Complex**: Symbols are multifaceted, connecting disparate domains in ways that can trigger insight

### Symbolic Resonance

The process by which engaging with a symbol creates a feedback loop:

1. Focus on the symbol integrates aspects of the self
2. This integration enables deeper perception of reality through the symbol
3. Deeper perception transforms the self further
4. The transformed self can perceive even more deeply
5. This creates an anagogic (upward-leading) spiral

### Mythos and Sacredness

**Mythos** is the combination of:
- **Ritual**: Enactive analogy and anagoge - processes that activate transformation machinery
- **Symbols**: Participatory metaphors that enable holding and engaging with the transcendent
- **Story**: Narrative structures that engage perspectival and participatory knowing

**Sacredness** emerges when Mythos is used to activate and seriously play with Religio (our fundamental relevance realization machinery).

### The Sacred vs Sacredness

Vervaeke distinguishes two views:

**Traditional View (The Sacred)**:
- Proposes something with absolute, supernatural value
- Claims there is an "essence to relevance" inhering in a particular object/being
- Suggests stable, complete, finished understanding is possible

**Alternative View (Process-based)**:
- Sacredness is the experience of inexhaustibility
- Both reality and relevance realization are combinatorially explosive
- No final formula or essence exists
- The Sacred = the transjective relationship between the inexhaustible nature of reality and the inexhaustible process of relevance realization

## Examples from the Episode

### The Scales of Justice
- Holding the symbol of scales activates cerebellar machinery
- This machinery coordinates complex contingencies
- Engaging with the symbol cultivates actual skills of justice
- The symbol discloses aspects of justice (e.g., balance as process, not state)

### Breath in Meditation
- The breath becomes a symbol for impermanence and interconnectedness
- Focusing on breath integrates aspects of the self
- The breath also discloses the processual nature of mind
- Creates symbolic resonance between self-integration and reality-disclosure

### The Flag and Patriotism
- Looking at a flag integrates various aspects of self (emotions, memories, associations)
- This integration process *is* the participation in patriotism
- The symbol enables becoming more patriotic through engagement

### Plato as Sacred
- Reading Plato transforms the reader
- Going into the world with Platonic insights transforms experience
- Returning to Plato reveals new insights
- This inexhaustible cycle demonstrates sacredness without supernatural claims

## Philosophical Implications

### Category Mistake in Traditional Accounts
The idea that some "thing" is essentially, absolutely, always relevant confuses:
- **Products** of relevance realization (what we find relevant)
- **Process** of relevance realization (how relevance is realized)

### The Nothingness of Reality and Self
- Reality is ultimately "no-thing-ness" - it cannot be fully framed
- The "I" that frames can never be captured in any frame
- There is deep participatory identification between these two inexhaustibilities

## Practical Applications

### Cultivating Symbolic Engagement
1. Identify symbols that resonate personally
2. Practice "serious play" with symbols rather than mere observation
3. Allow for the bi-directional flow: self-integration and reality-disclosure
4. Be open to transformation rather than seeking fixed understanding

### Recognizing Sacredness
- Look for experiences of inexhaustibility rather than absolute value
- Notice when engagement creates ongoing transformation
- Allow for the "not-knowing" that genuine sacredness involves

## Episode 35 Notes

### Key References

**Rudolf Otto - The Idea of the Holy**

Otto's analysis of the numinous experience - the sense of the wholly other that combines fascination and terror, underlies much of Vervaeke's discussion of how sacredness engages our deepest cognitive machinery.

**Lakoff & Johnson - Metaphors We Live By**

The foundational work on conceptual metaphor, showing how embodied experience grounds abstract thought. Vervaeke builds on this but argues for bi-directionality (activation, not just projection).

**Michael Polanyi - Personal Knowledge**

Polanyi's distinction between subsidiary and focal awareness is crucial for understanding how symbols work - we look *through* subsidiary elements to integrate them onto the focal object.

**Ramachandran - The Tell-Tale Brain**

The Buba/Kiki effect demonstrates how deeply we engage in cross-modal symbolic processing without conscious awareness.

**Jonathan Pageau - The Symbolic World**

Contemporary exploration of how symbolic thinking pervades human cognition and culture.
